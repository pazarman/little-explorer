# Outside review — 2026-09-06

Scored the way a games or kids-app critic would: by playing it cold, against the market,
without reference to our own rubric. `docs/skills/01-product-quality-bar.md` is how we decide
what is good enough to ship; this is what someone who has never read it would say.

Method: a cold first run with empty storage on a 393×851 phone, through onboarding into
several games, the celebration, the sticker book and Dress Up. Timings and screen-occupancy
figures are measured, not impressions.

## 6.5 / 10

| Axis | Score |
|---|---|
| Navigation & structure | 9 |
| Values & trust | 9 |
| Content breadth | 8 |
| Art direction | 5 |
| Pacing & game feel | 5 |
| Screen composition | 4 |
| Audio | 4 |
| Onboarding | 4 |

**The thinking is a tier above the execution.** The map, the no-fail stance, the adaptive
difficulty and the trilingual care are decisions a good studio would make. The screens they
are wrapped in are not yet at that level — and the gap is almost entirely composition and
art, which is the most fixable kind of gap there is.

## What it gets right

**The world map beats the competition's.** Khan Academy Kids and Lingokids use scrolling
shelves; Sago Mini uses an app grid. This uses *place* — six islands with fixed homes, a
causeway with stepping stones, a buddy that walks ahead of you. A two-year-old navigates it
by memory and colour. It is the one screen that looks like someone had a vision.

**The value system is close to unimpeachable.** No ads, no IAP, no account, no streaks, no
timers, no lives, no leaderboards. Offline. Free. Difficulty adapts silently rather than
grading her. In a category where the biggest names run daily-streak mechanics at
four-year-olds, that is a real position.

**Trilingual, and not lazily** — Spanish with correct gendered articles, Cantonese with proper
measure words, and it refuses to show Cantonese text when it has no Cantonese voice to speak.

**38 activities** is a lot of content for a free app.

## What holds it back

**Most screens are close to empty.** Measured across all 35 levels at tier 0: **average 15%
foreground occupancy, with 20 games under 15%.** The opening round of the whole game is one
present alone in a field of blue. Dolphin Dive — our own reference build — is 2%. Toca Boca's
entire craft is density; here a child's eye lands mostly on nothing.

**The art is three languages in one frame** — drawn SVG (good), platform emoji, and untextured
flat primitives (the "moon" is a pale ellipse; the seabed is emoji on a line).

**The celebration is the most chaotic screen in the app**, which is the wrong screen for that.
Eight unrelated confetti emoji including pizza slices in an ocean game; text drawn over moving
art so a button rendered as **"Wor!d map"**; the finished game still visible behind it.

**There is real dead air.** **2,676ms** from a correct answer to the next playable round,
against about two taps of play. Roughly half a session is the app talking while she waits.

**The sticker book is the weakest screen** — "Park" is a row of same-sized emoji on an empty
gradient, and it is meant to be the creative surface that accumulates.

**Dress Up has no arms or legs.** The face is the best art in the app and the skin tones are
genuinely inclusive; you just cannot put trousers on anyone.

**Onboarding asks the adult two questions before anyone plays** — type a name, pick an age
band — in an app whose premise is that nobody can read.

## Where it sits

Against **Sago Mini** and **Toca Boca** it loses on craft and moment-to-moment delight.
Against **Khan Academy Kids** it loses on production value and wins on navigation and on not
being an account-gated funnel. Against **Duolingo ABC** it wins decisively on ethics and loses
on polish. Its closest relative is **Pok Pok** — the calm, no-fail, respect-the-child end of
the market — whose entire proposition is craft, which is exactly where this is thinnest.

## Recommendation

**Yes, with a caveat.** For a parent who wants something free, offline, ad-free and genuinely
non-manipulative for a 2–4 year old, this is an easy recommendation and there is little else
like it. To a parent whose child has played Toca Boca, it will look homemade within a minute.

## Correction to this review

The audio score of 4 is unfair to the *craft*: the five cues are well made — layered voices,
real filter envelopes, a deliberately soft "wrong" tock. The failing is **coverage**: five
cues and three tunes across thirty-eight games, and no ambience anywhere. The score stands;
the reason is different, and `CRAFT-BACKLOG.md` A1 reflects that.
